\# AR vs VR vs MR Comparison



\## Overview



Augmented Reality (AR), Virtual Reality (VR), and Mixed Reality (MR) are major areas of Extended Reality (XR). Although they all involve digital content, they differ in how they combine virtual and physical environments.



\## Comparison



| Feature | Augmented Reality | Virtual Reality | Mixed Reality |

|---|---|---|---|

| Real environment | Visible | Mostly replaced | Visible |

| Digital content | Overlaid | Fully immersive | Integrated with environment |

| Immersion | Low to Medium | High | High |

| Interaction | Real-world + digital overlay | Virtual environment | Physical + virtual objects |

| Common devices | Smartphones, AR glasses | VR headsets | MR headsets |

| Tracking requirement | Position/image tracking | Head and controller tracking | Spatial/environment tracking |

| Example | AR navigation | VR simulation | Virtual object placed on a real table |



\## Augmented Reality



AR enhances the user's view of the physical environment by adding digital information or virtual objects.



\### Example



A mobile application can use a camera to display a virtual 3D object on a real-world surface.



\## Virtual Reality



VR replaces or surrounds the user's physical environment with a computer-generated environment.



\### Example



A VR training simulator can place a user inside a completely virtual laboratory or industrial environment.



\## Mixed Reality



MR combines physical and digital environments so that virtual objects can understand and interact with the surrounding physical space.



\### Example



A virtual 3D model can appear on a physical table and remain anchored to that location as the user moves around it.



\## Key Difference



The simplest way to understand the three technologies is:



\*\*AR → Adds digital content to reality\*\*



\*\*VR → Replaces reality with a virtual environment\*\*



\*\*MR → Combines physical and virtual environments with spatial interaction\*\*



\## Conclusion



AR, VR, and MR represent different approaches to immersive computing. Understanding their differences is important when selecting an appropriate technology for an XR application.
